StoryTalakad
via the Palace

A curse, and a dynasty with no sons

Start with the thing nobody puts on the signboard. In 1610 a widowed queen named Alamelamma was pursued to the Kaveri at Talakad over a set of temple jewels. Before she went into the river, the story goes, she cursed the Wadiyars: let Talakad turn to sand, let Malangi become a whirlpool, and let the kings of Mysuru never have children of their own.

Talakad is buried in sand today. And the Wadiyar line has adopted an heir in almost every generation since. Keep that in your head while you walk through the palace.

As the legend goes · Karnataka Gazetteer, Mysore District

FactMysore Palace

The fourth palace on this spot

Three earlier palaces stood here and burned. The one you're looking at was finished in 1912 by Henry Irwin, and it is deliberately a mixture — Hindu domes, Islamic arches, Rajput columns, Gothic proportions. On Sunday nights just under a hundred thousand bulbs come on for about an hour.

Archaeological Survey of India · verified 27 Aug 2026

TasteSayyaji Rao Road

The sweet that got its name here

Mysore pak was invented in this palace kitchen by a cook named Kakasura Madappa, improvising for the maharaja with gram flour, ghee and sugar. His family still runs a shop on Sayyaji Rao Road. Ask for the softer kind unless you like it crumbly.

Guru Sweet Mart, Mysuru

Mysore Palace

Amba Vilas · finished 1912 · Henry Irwin. Twelve minutes of material in the library; you choose how much of it you want.

Three palaces burned before this one

The wooden palace that stood here caught fire during a royal wedding in 1897. Rather than rebuild in wood again, the regent Maharani Kempananjammanni commissioned a stone one and gave the job to Henry Irwin, the British consulting architect for Madras. It took fifteen years and about forty-one lakh rupees of the time.

Archaeological Survey of India · verified 27 Aug 2026

SensoryDurbar Hall

Stand at the far left pillar and look up

Most people walk the centre of the Durbar Hall and photograph straight ahead. Move to the left-hand row instead. From there the stained glass ceiling — peacock motifs, shipped from Glasgow — lines up with the arches, and you can see how the cast-iron columns were made to look like carved stone.

Mysore Palace Board

StoryTalakad

Why there is almost always an adopted heir

In 1610 Alamelamma, widow of the Vijayanagara viceroy at Srirangapatna, was pursued to the Kaveri over a set of temple jewels. The story says she cursed the Wadiyars before she went into the water: sand for Talakad, a whirlpool at Malangi, and no sons for the kings of Mysuru. Talakad is buried in sand. The adoption record is real. Make of it what you like — but the family took it seriously enough to install her image in the palace.

As the legend goes · Karnataka Gazetteer, Mysore District

PracticalEntrance

Shoes, lockers, and the fee that changed

Entry is ₹120 for Indian adults as of 27 August 2026 — several sites still list ₹50 or ₹150, so carry the higher amount. Shoes come off at the entrance and there are free lockers to the left. Photography is not allowed inside the main halls. Closed on no weekday; the Sunday illumination runs about 7:00 to 8:00 pm.

Verified against Mysore Palace Board · 27 Aug 2026
