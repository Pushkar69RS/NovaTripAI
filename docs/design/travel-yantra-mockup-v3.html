The queen who went into the river

In 1610, a widowed queen named Alamelamma was pursued to the banks of the Kaveri at Talakad. The new rulers of Mysuru wanted the temple jewels in her keeping. The story goes that she stood at the edge of the water and cursed them before she went in. Let Talakad turn to sand. Let Malangi become a whirlpool. Let the kings of Mysuru never bear children.

Talakad today is buried under dunes of sand. And the Wadiyar family has, generation after generation, adopted its heirs. Inside the palace there is an image of Alamelamma, and the family honours it to this day. They did not take chances.

As the legend goes · Karnataka Gazetteer, Mysore District

TasteSayyaji Rao Road

The sweet invented in this kitchen

Mysore pak was improvised in the palace kitchen by a cook named Kakasura Madappa, who had gram flour, ghee and sugar to hand and a king waiting. Asked what it was called, he looked at it and said pak. His family still runs a small shop on Sayyaji Rao Road, and it usually has a queue. Ask for the soft kind. The crumbly one is for people who like their sweets to fight back.

Guru Sweet Mart, Mysuru

Three palaces burned before this one

The palace in front of you is the fourth one to stand on this ground. The one before it was mostly wood, and in 1897 it caught fire in the middle of a royal wedding. The family got out. The building did not. The regent queen, Kempananjammanni, decided there would be no more wooden palaces, and gave the job to Henry Irwin, the British consulting architect down in Madras. Stone, iron, and glass this time. It took fifteen years. So when you walk in, remember you are walking through someone's answer to a fire.

Mysore Palace Board

SensoryKalyana
Mantapa

Stand under the peacocks

In the wedding hall, most people look straight ahead and keep moving. Stop instead, and look up. The ceiling is stained glass, turquoise and green, worked into peacock patterns, and it was made in Glasgow along with the cast iron pillars holding it up. Scottish foundry work, shipped across the sea, to sit above Mysuru weddings. Then look at a pillar up close. It is iron pretending to be carved stone, and it pretends well.

Mysore Palace Board

The queen who went into the river

In 1610, a widowed queen named Alamelamma was pursued to the Kaveri at Talakad over a set of temple jewels. The story goes that she cursed the Wadiyars before she went into the water: sand for Talakad, a whirlpool at Malangi, and no children for the kings of Mysuru. Talakad is buried in sand. The adoption record is real. And inside this palace, the family keeps an image of Alamelamma and honours it to this day. Make of that what you like. They did not take chances.

As the legend goes · Karnataka Gazetteer, Mysore District

PracticalAt the gate

Fees, shoes, and the light switch

Entry is one hundred and twenty rupees for Indian adults, checked on the twenty-seventh of August 2026. Some websites still say fifty and some say a hundred and fifty, so trust the counter, not the internet. Shoes off at the entrance, free lockers to the left, and no photos inside the main halls. The famous illumination is Sunday evenings, roughly seven to eight. The lights come on all at once, not gradually. People gasp. Every time.
