[11] SLIDE 2 — Problem Statement
[12] Speaker: Pushkar  |  Duration: ~1.5 minutes
[13] Thank you Nithin, Let me begin by painting you a picture. Imagine you want to plan a 5-day trip to Rajasthan. Where do you start?
[14] You open MakeMyTrip for flights. Then switch to Booking.com for hotels. Then pull up TripAdvisor for attractions. Then Google Maps to figure out if your hotel is even near the fort. Then you call your cousin who visited last year... and still end up unsure.
[15] This fragmented experience is the core problem. Studies show travellers spend 6–10 hours just assembling a single itinerary across multiple disconnected platforms. That's a full workday — wasted on logistics.
[16] Now consider the Indian context specifically. India has 22 official languages and over 700 million people who are more comfortable communicating in their regional language than in English. Yet virtually all existing travel AI tools — ChatGPT, Gemini, even Google's travel features — work in English.
[17] [To panel:] If I may ask — have any of you tried asking a travel chatbot a question in Kannada? The results are... let's say, creative. [Brief pause]
[18] And once you're actually at a destination? There's no real-time intelligent guide to tell you the history of that temple, suggest the best local food nearby, or help you navigate — unless you can afford a human guide.
[19] Our target users are domestic Indian tourists — particularly non-English speakers, international visitors, solo travellers, and families. These are people who deserve the same quality of travel experience regardless of the language they speak.

[28] SLIDES 4 & 5 — Literature Survey (Tables)
[29] Speaker: Rohan Balu  |  Duration: ~1.5 minutes for both table slides
[30] I'll now walk you through our literature survey. We reviewed 12 key papers spanning AI planning systems, multilingual NLP, recommendation engines, and travel technology. Let me highlight the most impactful ones.
[31] The most critical paper for our domain is TravelPlanner by Xie et al. (2024), which is reference number 1(Point to the presentation). They built a benchmark with 1,225 real travel planning queries and tested GPT-4, Gemini, and other top models on it. The result was sobering — GPT-4 achieved less than 1% final pass rate on complex multi-constraint travel plans. Let that sink in. 1%.
[32] [To panel:] So when someone says 'just use ChatGPT for your travel plans,' the academic evidence suggests otherwise. Our system is designed specifically to address those failure modes.
[33] The second key paper is Singh et al.'s Travel Optix (2024), Reference number 2 (Point to presentation) which used CrewAI multi-agent systems to generate itineraries in under 5 minutes — and outperformed standalone GPT-4 and Gemini. This validates our multi-agent approach. But they had no multilingual support and no voice interface — gaps that Travel Yantra directly fills.
[34] For our NLP backbone, we drew from BERT (Devlin et al., 2019) and GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020), Reference number 3, for language understanding. For our multilingual component — arguably our biggest technical differentiator — we rely on IndicTrans2 (Gala et al., 2023), Reference number 4 from AI4Bharat, which achieves BLEU scores of 20-30+ across Indian languages. For our recommendation engine, we use Neural Collaborative Filtering from He et al. (2017), Reference number 9, and for route optimisation, we implement Dijkstra's classic algorithm.
[35] The multi-agent coordination strategy in our system is informed by AutoGen (Wu et al., 2023) reference number 6 and the ReAct framework (Yao et al., 2023) which enables our agents to interleave reasoning and tool use effectively.

[46] SLIDE 8 — Proposed System Overview
[47] Speaker: Pushkar  |  Duration: ~45 seconds
[48] Travel Yantra is a unified AI travel ecosystem. Think of it like the Avengers — but for travel planning. [Small smile] Four specialised agents, each an expert in their domain, coordinated by a central orchestration layer.
[49] The Itinerary Agent handles planning. The Accommodation Agent handles hotels. The Transportation Agent handles routes and bookings. The Cultural Guidance Agent answers your questions at the site — in your language.

[54] SLIDE 10 — System Architecture
[55] Speaker: Rohan Balu  |  Duration: ~45 seconds
[56] The architecture has three distinct layers. The input layer captures user preferences through whichever channel they choose — web, WhatsApp, or voice. This feeds into the processing layer — the multi-agent AI engine orchestrated by n8n.
[57] n8n acts like a conductor in an orchestra. It coordinates the four agents, manages API calls, handles errors gracefully, and ensures the system doesn't break if one API is temporarily unavailable. This is what makes our system fault-tolerant.
[58] The data layer uses Supabase — an open-source PostgreSQL database with real-time sync. This is what enables persistent user profiles. When you come back for your next trip, the system already knows you prefer budget accommodations, vegetarian restaurants, and cultural sites over beaches.

[60] SLIDE 11 — Methodology
[61] Speaker: Rohan Balu  |  Duration: ~30 seconds
[62] Our methodology combines four algorithms. LLM with Chain-of-Thought prompting ensures the LLM follows constraints like a chef following a recipe — step by step, not all at once. Neural Collaborative Filtering personalises recommendations based on your travel profile similarity with others. Dijkstra's algorithm — yes, the classic from 1959 — is still the gold standard for multi-stop route optimisation. And IndicTrans2 handles our multilingual translation with state-of-the-art Indic language accuracy.
[63] We'll evaluate using Constraint Satisfaction Rate and Commonsense Violation Rate for itinerary quality, BLEU and METEOR scores for translation, and P95 latency for system performance.

[86] ANTICIPATED Q&A — Preparation Notes
[87] Q: How is your system different from just using ChatGPT?
[88] A: ChatGPT is a single LLM with no real-time data, no booking capability, and no persistence. Our system is a 4-agent architecture with real-time API integration, multilingual support, booking links, WhatsApp access, voice interface, and user profile memory. The TravelPlanner paper (2024) showed GPT-4 achieves <1% success on complex travel planning — our specialized architecture is designed specifically to overcome those failures.
[89] Q: How accurate is the multilingual translation?
[90] A: We use IndicTrans2 from AI4Bharat, which achieves BLEU scores of 20-30+ across Indian language pairs on FLORES-200 benchmark. For travel-specific vocabulary, we'll fine-tune on domain data. Our target is 85%+ task-level accuracy, measured via BLEU and METEOR scores.
[91] Q: What about data privacy? User travel data is sensitive.
[92] A: We use Supabase with Row-Level Security (RLS) — each user can only access their own data. Authentication is JWT-based with token expiry. API keys are stored in environment variables, never in code. We comply with standard data protection practices and can implement additional encryption at rest.
[93] Q: What is the novelty of your project?
[94] A: The novelty lies in three areas: (1) Integration — no existing system combines these four channels with multi-agent AI for travel. (2) Language — we are the first to deploy IndicTrans2 specifically for conversational travel guidance in 5 Indian languages. (3) Architecture — the combination of n8n + Vapi + WhatsApp Business API for this domain is a novel open-architecture approach.
